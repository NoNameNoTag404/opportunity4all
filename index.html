Information Technology (IT)

Overview

The IT strand prepares students for software development, web design, cybersecurity, and multimedia production. Students gain practical skills to build digital solutions.

Skills Developed

Programming

Write code in modern languages to create apps and tools.

Web Development

Build responsive websites and dynamic web applications.

Database Management

Organize, manage, and secure digital information effectively.

Cybersecurity

Learn to protect systems from threats and vulnerabilities.

UI/UX Design

Create intuitive and user-friendly interfaces for apps and websites.

Problem Solving

Develop logical thinking to troubleshoot and optimize systems.

Opportunities

Internships

Work in tech companies to apply your skills in real projects.

Freelance Projects

Build small applications or websites for clients and portfolios.

Collaborative Development

Join team projects to learn teamwork and version control.

Game Development

Design, develop, and publish interactive games online.

Career Pathways

  • Software Developer: Build and maintain software applications for various industries.
  • Web Designer: Design visually appealing and functional websites.
  • System Administrator: Manage and maintain IT infrastructure in organizations.
  • IT Support Specialist: Troubleshoot technical issues and assist users.
  • Game Developer: Create and publish video games for entertainment and learning.

Platforms

  • Github – showcase coding projects
  • Fueler – professional portfolio building
  • Itch.io – publish digital projects and games

Humanities and Social Sciences (HUMSS)

Overview

HUMSS focuses on communication, research, and social awareness. Students explore human behavior, society, and public issues.

Skills Developed

Academic Writing

Produce structured essays and research papers effectively.

Public Speaking

Communicate ideas clearly in front of audiences.

Critical Thinking

Analyze, evaluate, and synthesize information logically.

Research Analysis

Gather, interpret, and present data on social issues.

Communication Skills

Enhance written and verbal communication for various contexts.

Social Awareness

Understand societal issues and advocate for positive change.

Competitions & Awards

  • Wharton Global Youth Comment & Win
  • GRC Social Impact Essay Competition
  • Golden Pen Awards

Opportunities

NGO Internships – build real-world communication skills.
Research Conferences – present and network professionally.
Community Programs – participate in social initiatives.
Communications Internships – content creation and media work.

Career Pathways

  • Law: Work as a lawyer or legal consultant advocating for justice.
  • Journalism: Report, write, and publish stories for media outlets.
  • Education: Teach, mentor, and guide future generations.
  • Psychology: Study human behavior and provide mental health support.
  • Public Administration: Manage government programs and community projects.
  • Social Work: Assist communities with development and welfare initiatives.

Accountancy, Business and Management (ABM)

Overview

ABM prepares students for entrepreneurship, financial management, and leadership. It develops business thinking and management skills for real-world application.

Skills Developed

Financial Literacy

Manage budgets, investments, and understand financial markets.

Entrepreneurship

Start and manage businesses while learning operational skills.

Marketing Strategy

Create effective plans to promote products and services.

Business Communication

Write reports, emails, and communicate professionally.

Leadership

Lead teams and manage projects with responsibility.

Business Planning

Develop actionable plans to grow ventures successfully.

Programs

Business Skills Pass – practical learning for students.
Company of the Year – entrepreneurship competition.
JA Trade Challenge – business strategy contests.
Blue Ocean Competition – global business plan challenge.

Career Pathways

  • Entrepreneur: Start and grow businesses while managing risks effectively.
  • Accountant: Handle financial records, taxes, and reporting for organizations.
  • Financial Analyst: Analyze markets and investments to guide decisions.
  • Business Manager: Oversee operations, teams, and strategic planning.
  • Marketing Specialist: Promote products/services and understand consumer behavior.

Technical Vocational Livelihood (TVL)

Overview

TVL provides hands-on training and practical workplace experience. Students gain technical competencies for employment in diverse industries.

Training Areas

Technical Maintenance

Learn repair and maintenance for electronics and machinery.

Hospitality Services

Gain skills in hotel, food, and customer service management.

ICT Support

Support computer systems, networks, and software applications.

Industrial Skills

Acquire competencies in factory and workshop operations.

Home Economics

Learn culinary, sewing, and lifestyle management skills.

Service Operations

Handle logistics, operations, and customer service professionally.

Work Immersion

Participate in real workplaces to develop discipline, collaboration, and technical skills.

Certifications

TESDA Certification – official skill validation.
Technical Workshops – hands-on industry learning.
Industry Training – internships in local companies.
Skills Development Programs – specialized vocational courses.

Career Pathways

  • Technician: Perform skilled technical tasks in various industries.
  • Service Specialist: Provide professional services in hospitality or technical fields.
  • Technical Assistant: Support engineers and skilled professionals in projects.
  • Skilled Worker: Apply practical training in real-world work environments.
